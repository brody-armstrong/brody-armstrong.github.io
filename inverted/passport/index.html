Has this worked before?

The rules were written and committed locally at 16:23 PT on 18 November 2026, before any outcome data was fetched; first public when pushed to GitHub (time added after the push). A local commit's date is set by whoever makes it, so the push is the first time anyone else can check, and it came after the outcome data was fetched. The rules: train on the 2022 and 2023 NEXT Pro cohorts, test on 2024 (outcomes through 2026), and use the new model only if it beats both simpler baselines on both scores.

On the 2024 test cohort (370 players), the readiness model had lower Brier score and log loss than both simpler baselines, so the page uses it.

Exploratory: players with no MLS minutes yet

This check was not pre-registered; it was added after the backtest. The model uses MLS minutes a player has already had, so some of its advantage could simply be that players already in MLS stay in MLS. Among the 278 test players with no MLS minutes at all (21 of them reached 500), the readiness model still scored best on both measures. The margin is smaller: against minutes alone the 90% interval for the log-loss difference excludes zero; against the raw NEXT Pro percentile it does not (−0.047 to +0.002). For these players the model is ahead of the simpler baselines, but not clearly ahead of the raw percentile.

How it is built

  • Data: American Soccer Analysis public API, MLS NEXT Pro match by match (2022–2026, regular season) and MLS minutes and g+ by season. Thanks to ASA for making it public. Birth dates are ASA's (checked against the club's announcements where they give one).
  • Shrinkage: each KPI is pulled toward the average for the player's position group, more strongly the noisier his own match-to-match record. The interval is a 90% empirical-Bayes interval.
  • Age-adjusted percentile: where the estimate sits among players of the same position and season after allowing for age.
  • Readiness model: a season-by-season model of the chance of crossing 500 MLS minutes, from age, shrunk g+, NEXT Pro minutes, MLS minutes already given and position. Intervals come from refitting it on 500 resamples of players, with each player's own g+ drawn from its interval.
  • Baselines: the raw NEXT Pro g+ percentile alone, and minutes alone.
  • MLS projection: pathway model (published).
Limits
  • Reaching 500 MLS minutes depends on coaching decisions, depth charts, injuries and the first team's needs, not only on the player.
  • One test cohort (2024, 370 players). The result could look different in another year.
  • Public event-derived data only: no tracking, physical or medical data.
  • The model learns from what happened to past players, including who clubs chose to promote. It describes those patterns; it does not show what causes readiness.
  • Birth dates come from ASA rather than official rosters, which do not publish them in a usable form; three were checked against club announcements and matched.
