Why does MLS stay so competitive?

MLS is built for parity: a salary cap, limited star slots, no relegation. This project measures how much that shows up on the pitch, where it comes from, and what it means for a club deciding where to spend.

Each question below leads to the next. Every level has its own page, and the simulator is one click away from all of them.

How it was built

I work where applied AI meets football analysis: statistical models, machine learning and the software that makes them usable. The work moves in loops of build, debug, rewrite and learn, with AI tools used for coding, review and checking, and every result traced back to code and data that anyone can rerun.

  • Predictions before data. For Paper 2, the predictions were written before the analysis data was downloaded and recorded in the project log.
  • Checks that fail are reported. Paper 1 ran 16 validation checks: 8 passed, 7 failed, 1 partly passed. Paper 2's main mechanism was not supported, and the paper says so.
  • Checked against the market. Ratings are compared with closing betting odds on 5,987 MLS matches, and the simulator with preseason futures.
  • Rebuilt in public. The simulator was rebuilt on public data only and validated against the original version before release.
  • Nov 12 Paper 2 predictions recorded; Paper 2 frozen
  • Nov 13 Simulator v1.1 validated on public data
  • Nov 16 Code and papers made public; 2026 playoff forecast computed
